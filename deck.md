## Presentation Structure: Causal Modelling in Procurement - Fragrance Supply Chain Focus

### 1. Opening Context (1.5 minutes)
- **The data transformation journey**: Successfully transitioning to data-driven procurement through predictive analytics and linear optimisation
- **The volatile landscape**: Health & Beauty sector facing unprecedented complexity:
  - Natural fragrance ingredients sourced from politically unstable regions (Bulgarian rose, Indian sandalwood)
  - Climate change disrupting traditional harvest patterns in Provence and Morocco
  - Sustainability regulations driving reformulation pressures
  - Competitors stockpiling scarce ingredients, distorting market signals
- **Current capabilities**: ML forecasting and optimisation providing predictions, but missing the "why"
- **The critical gap**: When rose oil prices spike 40%, is it Bulgarian elections, drought, or Chinese perfume demand?
- **The proposition**: Causal intelligence layer that transforms predictions into strategic insight

### 2. What is Causal Modelling? (1.5 minutes)
- **Definition**: Framework for understanding cause-and-effect relationships, not just correlations
- **Fragrance example**: Rose oil prices correlate with Bulgarian political stability index, but the causal path flows through harvest labour availability → processing capacity → export volumes
- **Core components**: 
  - Directed Acyclic Graphs mapping ingredient supply chains
  - Counterfactual analysis ("what if France had normal rainfall?")
  - Intervention planning (quantifying impact of direct sourcing)

### 3. Why It Matters: Strategic Value for Procurement (2.5 minutes)
- **Competitive advantage**: Act on root causes whilst competitors react to symptoms
- **Risk disambiguation**: Distinguish temporary harvest delays from structural supply shifts
- **Scenario planning**: Model cascading effects of India's sandalwood export restrictions
- **Supplier negotiations**: Understand true cost drivers beyond "market conditions"
- **Illustrative value creation** (example figures for demo purposes only):
  - ~15% reduction in premium fragrance ingredient costs through pre-emptive sourcing
  - 3-4 week advantage in securing alternative supplies during disruptions
  - Multi-million annual savings potential in the fragrance category through optimised buying strategies
  - ~25% reduction in reformulation emergencies

### 4. Practical Applications (2.5 minutes)
- **Enhanced ML outputs**: Causal layer explains *why* prices will move, not just predicting *that* they will
- **Optimisation with context**: Linear optimiser recommendations enriched with causal understanding (e.g., "buy now because X drives Y")
- **Knowledge graph integration**: Codifying buyer expertise into causal relationships stored within existing infrastructure
- **Local knowledge preservation**: Transform tacit buyer knowledge into explicit causal models that persist beyond individual tenure
- **Geopolitical intelligence**: Map how tensions translate into specific commodity impacts across all categories
- **Tariff impact modelling**: Understand cascading effects beyond direct cost increases on imported ingredients
- **Supply chain resilience**: Identify which disruptions are localised vs systemic across the supplier base
- **Sustainability transitions**: Model the causal impact of switching to sustainable alternatives

### 5. Illustrative Example: Bulgarian Rose Crisis (1.5 minutes)
- **Scenario**: October 2022 - ML model flags unusual rose oil price patterns
- **Traditional approach**: Assume seasonal fluctuation, maintain normal purchasing
- **Causal analysis reveals**: 
  - Political instability → harvest labour uncertainty → processors delaying investments → reduced capacity for 2023 season
  - Not a temporary spike but structural supply reduction
- **Prescriptive action**: 
  - Immediate: Secure Turkish and Moroccan alternatives
  - Strategic: Invest in Bulgarian supplier partnerships to ensure labour stability
- **Illustrative result**: Avoided price increase that hit competitors who reacted later

### 6. Implementation Approach (1 minute)
- **Phase 1**: Map causal pathways for top 5 fragrance ingredients (representing 80% of spend)
- **Phase 2**: Integrate weather data, political risk indices, and market intelligence into causal models
- **Phase 3**: Build causal inference layer connecting to the existing MLOps platform and knowledge graph
- **Phase 4**: Embed buyer expertise through continuous model validation and enrichment
- **Success metrics**: Response time to disruptions, forecast accuracy improvement, cost avoidance captured

This structure comprehensively shows how causal modelling enhances existing data capabilities whilst addressing broader procurement challenges beyond just the fragrance example.
